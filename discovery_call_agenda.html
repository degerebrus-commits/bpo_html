Section 1 · 0:00 – 3:00
Open & frame the session
⏱ 3 minutes — do not skip
Opening script
"Thanks for filling out the pre-audit — that already told me a lot. Today's call is structured as a diagnostic, not a pitch. I'm going to walk us through three specific checkpoints in your workflow, and by the end we'll have identified exactly where your pipeline is losing the most throughput. That gives us a clear, specific thing to fix — not a general sense that things could be better. Does that work for you?"
Listen for
A coach who says "yes, let's do it" without qualifiers is engaged and low-resistance. A coach who immediately pivots to their situation ("well before we do that, I should tell you about…") needs gentle redirection back to the framework — "let's capture that in context as we go through the gates."
Framing rule
Never call it a "sales call." Never say "I want to show you what I offer." The frame is: diagnostician running a process audit. Maintain this throughout. The offer comes from the diagnosis, not from you.

Section 2 · 3:00 – 8:00
Pre-audit data review
⏱ 5 minutes — anchor with their own numbers
Script
"Before we go deeper, I want to reflect back what your pre-audit told me. You're seeing roughly [X]% inquiry rate — which is actually solid, it means your marketing is working. The leak isn't at the top of the funnel. It's further downstream. Based on your numbers, I'm estimating you're leaving around $[X] on the table per month. I want to show you exactly which stage that's coming from."
Listen for
Surprise at the number — "really, that much?" — is a buying signal. Skepticism — "I'm not sure those numbers are right" — is an invitation to walk through the math together. Do it. The formula is transparent and it strengthens your authority.
Confirm their visitor and inquiry numbers match what they submitted. Discrepancies are useful — they reveal that the coach doesn't track these consistently, which is itself a finding.
Mirror their exact symptom language from the pre-audit checkboxes. If they ticked "I'm busy all day but my bank account doesn't show it" — use that phrase back to them verbatim.
Do not explain or diagnose yet. Just reflect. The goal of this section is to make them feel seen and to establish that your data-led approach is different from what they've experienced before.

Section 4 · 20:00 – 23:00
Constraint reveal
⏱ 3 minutes — the diagnostic verdict
Critical rule
State the constraint with confidence. Do not hedge ("it seems like maybe…"). Do not list multiple problems equally. One constraint, named clearly, with the revenue number attached. This is the moment where your authority is established or lost.
Reveal script — adapt gate name and numbers
"Based on what we've just gone through — your primary constraint is [Gate X]. Here's what that means in dollar terms: with [X] inquiries a month and a [$X] program, [leak description]. That's not a projection — it's your current throughput gap calculated from your own numbers. Everything else in your workflow can be optimised, but until this gate is fixed, those improvements won't show up in revenue."
Listen for
The ideal response is a pause, then "yeah, that's exactly it" or "I hadn't thought of it that way." That's the moment of recognition — it means the frame has landed. Silence after the reveal is positive. Do not fill it. Let the number sit.

Objection handling
⏱ Respond, then return to next step
Objection Response
"I need to think about it." "Completely fair. While you do — I'd ask you to track, just for one week, how many minutes you spend on [the constraint activity]. Most coaches come back and say the number surprised them."
Then: set a specific follow-up date before you hang up. "Should we speak again Thursday?"
"I don't have the budget right now." "Let me ask you this — what's one client worth at your current program price? [Pause.] That's the question on the table. Is this a budget issue or a priority issue?"
If genuine budget constraint: offer a payment plan. Two instalments, 50% on kick-off.
"I already have Calendly / Typeform." "Having the tool and having the system are two different things. The audit shows the tools exist but aren't connected in a way that removes the manual steps. That's exactly what the implementation does."
"Can't I just do this myself?" "You can. And if you do, I'd encourage you to time how long it takes. The package covers the build, the testing, the Zapier logic, and the configuration — not just knowing what to set up. Most coaches who try this themselves spend 15+ hours and still have gaps."
"What about the other two gates?" "Great question — that's Phase 2. TOC is explicit: fix the primary constraint first or improving the others won't show up in revenue. Once Gate [X] is stable, we come back and address Gate [Y]. I can quote you a bundled rate for both phases if you want to plan the full roadmap now."
"I need to speak to my partner / team." "Of course. What's the best way to include them — could we do a quick 15-minute call with both of you this week so I can walk through the numbers directly?"
Avoid email-only follow-ups for this objection — decisions made asynchronously rarely close.
Post-call actions
Send follow-up email within 2 hours: include the constraint name, the revenue leak figure, the package proposed, the price, and the exact next step.
Complete the Post-Audit Notes section of the BPO Framework document with call observations.
Add constraint pattern to your bottleneck database — note their industry, program price point, and which gate scored highest.
If no decision: calendar the follow-up before closing the browser.
